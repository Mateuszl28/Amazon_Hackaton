The offence has four attempts, called downs, to move the ball 10 yards; reaching them resets the count to first down.
On fourth down a team usually punts the ball away or tries a field goal rather than risk handing possession over where they stand.
A touchdown is worth 6 points, the extra-point kick after it 1, a two-point conversion 2, a field goal 3, a safety 2 for the defence.
The play ends when the ball carrier is tackled to the ground, steps out of bounds, or the pass falls incomplete.
An incomplete pass stops the clock; a run or a completed pass inside the field keeps it running.
False start: an offensive player moves before the snap. Five yards, and the play is blown dead before it begins.
Offside / encroachment: a defender is in or across the neutral zone at the snap. Five yards.
Holding by the offence: grabbing or hooking a defender who is not carrying the ball. Ten yards, and it often wipes out a big gain.
Defensive holding or illegal contact: restraining a receiver early in the route. Five yards and usually an automatic first down.
Pass interference: significant contact that stops a receiver playing a catchable ball. The offence gets the ball where the foul happened; on the defence it is an automatic first down.
Roughing the passer: hitting the quarterback late, in the head, or driving him into the ground. Fifteen yards and an automatic first down.
Targeting: a hit with the crown of the helmet or to a defenceless player's head. Fifteen yards, and in college the player is ejected.
Unsportsmanlike conduct or taunting: fifteen yards, enforced from where the previous play ended.
Delay of game: the play clock runs out before the snap. Five yards.
Intentional grounding: the quarterback throws the ball away from any receiver while under pressure inside the pocket. Loss of down and yardage.
A turnover means the other team takes possession: an interception is a pass caught by the defence, a fumble is a loose ball recovered by either side.
A yellow flag on the field means an official saw a foul; the referee then announces it and the penalty yardage is walked off.
Coaches may challenge some calls, and scoring plays and turnovers are reviewed automatically by a booth official.
College football differs from the NFL: one foot in bounds completes a catch, the clock stops briefly after a first down, and overtime is played from the opponent's 25-yard line.
